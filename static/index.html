Key Moments

1619
First Enslaved Africans in Virginia
The first recorded arrival of enslaved Africans in the English colony of Virginia marks the beginning of slavery in what would become the United States.
1791–1804
Haitian Revolution
Enslaved people in the French colony of Saint-Domingue revolt, leading to the establishment of Haiti — the first free Black republic.
1831
Nat Turner's Rebellion
One of the most significant slave rebellions in American history shakes the institution of slavery and intensifies the abolitionist movement.
1863
Emancipation Proclamation
President Abraham Lincoln declares enslaved people in Confederate states "shall be then, thenceforward, and forever free."
1865
Thirteenth Amendment
Slavery is formally abolished throughout the United States with the ratification of the Thirteenth Amendment to the Constitution.
1920s
Harlem Renaissance
A cultural and intellectual explosion in Harlem, New York, that redefines African American expression in literature, art, music, and thought.
1954
Brown v. Board of Education
The Supreme Court unanimously rules that racial segregation in public schools is unconstitutional, dismantling "separate but equal."
1955
Montgomery Bus Boycott
Rosa Parks' arrest sparks a year-long boycott that desegregates Montgomery's buses and launches Martin Luther King Jr. into national prominence.
1963
March on Washington
Over 250,000 people gather at the Lincoln Memorial where Dr. King delivers his legendary "I Have a Dream" speech.
1964
Civil Rights Act
President Lyndon B. Johnson signs the Civil Rights Act, outlawing discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin.
1965
Voting Rights Act
The Voting Rights Act prohibits racial discrimination in voting, removing barriers like literacy tests that had disenfranchised Black voters.
1990–1994
End of Apartheid
Nelson Mandela is released after 27 years and becomes South Africa's first Black president, ending decades of institutionalized racial segregation.
